Dynamic TF2 Coordinate Transformations & Kinematics

Mathematical frame alignment: [map] ➔ [odom] ➔ [base_link] ➔ [camera_link]

📐 Homogeneous Transformation Matrix

The position of a semantic detection in the global map frame is computed via:

$$P_{map} = T_{map}^{odom} \cdot T_{odom}^{base\_link} \cdot T_{base\_link}^{cam} \cdot P_{cam}$$

Where each transformation matrix $T \in SE(3)$ is structured as:

$$T = \begin{bmatrix} R(\mathbf{q}) & \mathbf{t} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Using unit quaternion rotation matrices $R(\mathbf{q})$ to guarantee singularity-free rotation without gimbal lock.

🔄 Coordinate Frame Chain Breakdown

  • map: Fixed global visual landmark world frame (Member 4 ORB-SLAM3).
  • odom: Local continuous odometry frame published by flight controller EKF.
  • base_link: Center of gravity of the drone body.
  • camera_link: Physical camera mounting point $[x=+0.12, y=0.00, z=+0.05]$m with $-15^\circ$ pitch tilt.
  • camera_optical_frame: Standard optical frame ($X$ Right, $Y$ Down, $Z$ Forward).

Q1: Why did you choose ROS 2 Jazzy instead of ROS 1 Noetic for this autonomous drone project?

Answer: "ROS 1 relies on a centralized roscore master node, creating a single point of failure that is dangerous for flight systems. In contrast, ROS 2 Jazzy utilizes the decentralized DDS (Data Distribution Service) middleware standard (OMG standard), which offers:

  • No Single Point of Failure: Peer-to-peer automatic node discovery.
  • Configurable QoS Policies: Ability to specify deterministic deadlines, reliability levels, and liveliness per-topic.
  • Native Real-Time Support: Predictable memory allocation and intra-process zero-copy communication.
  • Modern C++20 / Python 3.12+ Support: Long-term support (LTS) release through 2029."

Q2: How do you handle coordinate frame drift and offset between the MAVROS local_origin and the SLAM map frame?

Answer: "The flight controller's internal EKF estimates odometry in the odom frame, which experiences incremental drift over time. Member 4's Visual SLAM provides loop-closure corrected poses in the global map frame. As Member 3, I manage the tf2_ros transformation chain by computing $T_{map}^{odom} = T_{map}^{base\_link} \cdot (T_{odom}^{base\_link})^{-1}$. Whenever Visual SLAM performs a loop closure, only the transform $T_{map}^{odom}$ is updated, preserving continuity in high-rate 50Hz setpoints sent to the flight controller without causing mechanical jerks."

Q3: What happens if Visual SLAM loses tracking inside a featureless GPS-denied corridor?

Answer: "Our safety_failsafe_node runs a 50Hz watchdog checking the timestamp difference $\Delta t = t_{now} - t_{last\_slam}$. If $\Delta t > 350\text{ms}$:

  • 1. The safety supervisor immediately publishes a critical SafetyAlert.
  • 2. The state machine transitions from SEMANTIC_GOAL_NAV to EMERGENCY_HOLD.
  • 3. The bridge commands the flight controller to switch to altitude-hold / optical flow velocity damping to freeze the drone in place.
  • 4. A 3.0s relocalization window is initiated. If tracking is restored, navigation resumes; otherwise, the drone executes a controlled vertical landing."

Q4: How do you prevent setpoint queue buildup and sudden jerky movements when goals change?

Answer: "We employ a two-stage approach: First, on the ROS 2 transport layer, we use KEEP_LAST with a depth of 10 and VOLATILE durability to eliminate stale message backpressure. Second, in our system_integration_bridge, we run a continuous 50Hz Trajectory Smoother with jerk and acceleration limiters ($\le 1.2\text{ m/s}^2$). When a new waypoint is received from Member 1 or Member 5, the smoother dynamically recalculates a smooth velocity profile rather than jumping instantaneously."
